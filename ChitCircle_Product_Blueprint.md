# ChitCircle — Product Blueprint

**Version:** 1.0  
**Basis:** *ChitCircle: A PayPal-Powered Platform for Community Savings Circles Worldwide* (12-page concept document)  
**Purpose:** Turn the concept into a buildable product plan for the PayPal AI Hackathon.  
**Product name:** ChitCircle is a working name.

> **Product in one sentence:** ChitCircle helps community savings-circle organizers run collections, payout rules, auctions, records, and member communication from one shared system, with AI proposing actions and people approving anything that moves money.

## 1. Product decision

Build ChitCircle as an **organizer-first operating system for community savings circles**, not as a wallet, lender, or operator of the savings fund.

The product should solve one clear problem first: **remove repetitive work and arguments from running a savings circle while making the numbers and rules easy for members to verify.** PayPal is the payment and collection integration at the center. A deterministic settlement engine is the core business logic. AI agents assist with reminders, risk signals, and explanations, but do not control money on their own.

### Product promise

- Less manual chasing and bookkeeping for organizers.
- One shared record of contributions, bids, settlements, and payouts.
- Clear payout rules that the system applies consistently.
- A member can understand how their contribution or payout was calculated.
- Group-level status is visible without exposing every person's private financial details.
- The same configurable engine can support different savings-circle traditions and rules.

### What ChitCircle is not

For the hackathon prototype, ChitCircle is **software for organizers**. It must not claim to be licensed to run chit funds, to hold the pot, to remove the organizer's legal or financial responsibility, or to provide a verified real-world credit score. The prototype uses PayPal sandbox flows and simulated member behavior unless a real capability has been tested and approved.

## 2. Users and their main needs

### Primary user: circle organizer

The organizer currently tracks payments, follows up with late members, runs draws or auctions, calculates dividends, settles disputes, and worries about a winner who may stop paying later.

**Job to be done:** “Help me run the circle fairly and on time without turning every month into a manual admin job.”

The organizer needs:
- A quick view of who has paid, who is late, and what needs attention.
- Rules enforced by software rather than personal judgment.
- A queue of proposed actions with one-tap approval.
- Exact settlement math and an audit trail.
- Guarantor or collateral checks before a payout is released.
- A simple view of current and future obligations.

### Secondary user: circle member

The member needs to know what they owe, what they have paid, how the payout was calculated, what they may bid, and what remains due after receiving the pot.

**Job to be done:** “Help me trust the process, understand my own numbers, and check the group's progress without having to ask the organizer.”

The member needs:
- Their own payment and payout history.
- A clear contribution breakdown for each cycle.
- Current group status and auction results.
- Plain-language explanations based on the ledger.
- A way to flag a record for review.
- Privacy for their personal details and risk information.

### Product operator / support role (future)

This role may manage system configuration and investigate technical failures. It is not part of the minimum demo unless needed to support testing.

## 3. Core product principles

1. **Organizer-first:** Every major feature should remove a real organizer task.
2. **Rules before AI:** Settlement, eligibility, deadlines, and bid checks must be deterministic code, not generated by a language model.
3. **Humans approve money actions:** AI can draft, explain, flag, and recommend. It cannot independently release a payout, create a financial commitment, or change a member's obligations.
4. **One append-only record:** Financial events are added to the ledger. Corrections create new linked events rather than silently changing history.
5. **Clear, tiered visibility:** The group sees the information needed to trust the process; personal details and private risk data remain restricted.
6. **Configurable by group and jurisdiction:** A payout mode, cap, commission, tie-break, security rule, and visibility default should be settings, not hard-coded assumptions.
7. **Explain the math:** Every amount shown should be traceable to the rules and ledger events that produced it.
8. **Prototype honestly:** Do not imply that sandbox transactions are real funds or that untested cross-border or payout functionality works.

## 4. Product structure and main screens

### 4.1 Organizer dashboard

The home screen should answer four questions immediately: **What is due? Who needs attention? What is waiting for my approval? Is any payout at risk?**

Show:
- Active circles and current cycle.
- Collection progress: paid, pending, late.
- Auction status and next deadline.
- Actions awaiting approval: reminder, catch-up invoice, guarantor verification, payout-related step, member-risk alert.
- Risk alerts with clear reasons rather than unexplained scores.
- A short daily summary from the organizer copilot, if included.

### 4.2 Circle setup

The organizer creates a circle and configures:
- Circle name and currency.
- Members and invitations.
- Number of members and cycle frequency.
- Contribution / chit value and installment amount.
- Start date, cycle calendar, and auction deadline.
- Payout mode: fixed rotation, lottery, auction with dividend, or agreed discount. Interest-bearing advance is a later, jurisdiction-controlled option.
- Auction rules: sealed or open mode, discount cap, tie-break, no-bid fallback, eligibility, and whether members in arrears may bid.
- Commission and dividend rules.
- Guarantor/collateral requirements.
- Jurisdiction profile and applicable limits.
- Ledger visibility defaults.

The system should validate settings before the circle starts and show a readable summary for members to accept.

### 4.3 Circle timeline

Use a member-by-cycle timeline as a core visual. Each row is a member; each column is a cycle. Show contribution status, late/pending state, who received the pot, and the result of the auction or draw. The source document recommends Bryntum for this hero view.

For the hackathon, include a simulator that can fast-forward a 20-month circle in about one minute so the timeline fills with believable test data.

### 4.4 Cycle and auction workspace

Show:
- Contribution progress and current pot state.
- Eligible bidders and the remaining time.
- Bid mode and bid deadline.
- Valid/invalid bid feedback, without exposing sealed bids before close.
- Auction result, tie-break reason, discount, winner amount, commission, dividend pool, and per-member dividend.
- A final settlement preview before it is written to the ledger.

The settlement calculation should run in a pure, unit-tested service. The user interface displays the result; it must not calculate money independently.

### 4.5 Shared ledger

Every installment, invoice or payment match, bid, auction close, dividend calculation, payout state change, guarantor event, refund, and dispute should become a timestamped ledger event.

Use three visibility levels:

- **Group view:** cycle payment status, pot status, auction result, payout status, and the shared dividend breakdown.
- **Member private view:** the member's own complete transaction and obligation history, plus private risk/security details about themselves.
- **Organizer view:** the full circle record, security status, risk alerts, and dispute queue.

A flagged entry opens a dispute/review record. The original event remains visible to authorized users, with the review outcome stored separately.

### 4.6 Member home

Show the member's next due amount and date, payment status, own history, circle status, previous payout or bid details, remaining obligations, and a plain-language explanation area. The member should not need organizer access to understand their own numbers.

### 4.7 Approval inbox

All proposed actions should appear in one place with:
- What the system wants to do.
- Why it is suggested.
- The person and circle affected.
- The financial or privacy impact.
- The evidence or ledger events used.
- Approve, reject, or review-later actions.

Money-moving actions must require explicit approval and an idempotent backend action so repeated clicks cannot create duplicate payments.

## 5. Core workflows

### Flow A: Start a circle

1. Organizer creates a circle.
2. Organizer selects a payout mode and jurisdiction profile.
3. Organizer sets contributions, cycle dates, auction rules, commissions, dividends, visibility, and security requirements.
4. System validates the configuration and shows a plain-language rules summary.
5. Members are invited and accept the rules.
6. Circle becomes active when its setup conditions are met.

### Flow B: Collect contributions

1. A cycle opens and the system generates the expected contribution for each member.
2. PayPal subscription/billing or invoice flow is initiated, based on the tested integration path.
3. Payment callbacks or reconciliation jobs match PayPal transactions to the member and cycle.
4. The ledger records the payment event and the group view updates.
5. If a contribution remains unpaid, the collections agent proposes a polite reminder.
6. Later escalation may create a catch-up invoice and alert the organizer.
7. The organizer approves any action that sends a new request or has a financial effect.

### Flow C: Run an auction and settle the cycle

1. System checks payment status and bidder eligibility.
2. Eligible members who have not already won can submit valid bids.
3. The auction closes at the configured deadline.
4. The auction service applies the configured tie-break or no-bid fallback.
5. The settlement engine calculates the winner amount, organizer commission, dividend pool, each member's dividend, and net installment.
6. The app presents a settlement preview with a full breakdown.
7. The result is written to the ledger and shown to members.
8. The payout remains pending until the required security checks and supported payment path are confirmed.

### Flow D: Early payout by agreed discount

1. A member requests early access to the pot.
2. System checks eligibility, current security, and group-configured limits.
3. System proposes terms based on the group's rules and recent auction history; it explains the cost using the member's actual expected cash flows.
4. The group consent rule is applied: organizer approval, member vote, or both.
5. If accepted, the discount is treated as an uncontested bid and sent through the same settlement engine.
6. The reason and terms are clearly marked in the ledger.

**Product default recommendation:** use agreed discount for the demo rather than interest-bearing advances. Interest-bearing advances are closer to lending and need separate legal review and stricter controls.

### Flow E: Verify security before payout

1. The system opens a guarantor or collateral check for the winner.
2. Status moves through `pending → verified → released`.
3. The organizer sees the remaining obligations of the winning member and the reason for the required security level.
4. The payout remains blocked until all configured checks pass and the organizer approves the release step.
5. Every status change is recorded in the ledger.

The concept document raises an open question about whether this step should be on a case-by-case basis. Product recommendation: make it a configurable rule based on jurisdiction and circle policy, with risk-based escalation rather than an unreviewable AI decision.

### Flow F: Explain or challenge a record

1. A member asks a question such as, “Why is my installment 4,650 this month?”
2. The explainer retrieves the actual cycle ledger and settlement output.
3. It responds with the amounts and formula used, including links or references to the relevant entries.
4. A member can flag a ledger event.
5. The organizer reviews the event and adds a resolution record; the original entry is not silently overwritten.

## 6. Financial rules and settlement engine

The settlement engine is the product's most important correctness boundary. It should be a deterministic, separately tested service with no dependency on an AI model.

### 6.1 Auction chit rules from the source document

1. Every member is expected to pay the installment, defined in the source as chit value divided by the number of members.
2. Members who have not yet won may bid a discount off the chit value.
3. The highest valid discount wins.
4. The winner receives chit value minus the winning discount.
5. The organizer's configured commission is taken from the discount.
6. The remainder becomes the dividend pool and is shared across members according to the configured rule.
7. A member can win only once. In the final cycle, the remaining member receives the pot without a bid.

The document states that caps, commission, dividend rule, and tie-break should be configurable because they differ by group and jurisdiction.

### 6.2 Worked example in the source

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Chit value | 100,000 |
| Members | 20 |
| Regular installment | 5,000 |
| Winning discount | 12,000 |
| Winner receives | 88,000 |
| Organizer commission | 5,000 |
| Dividend pool | 7,000 |
| Dividend per member | 350 |
| Net installment that month | 4,650 |

The source reconciles the flow as `20 × 4,650 = 93,000`, equal to `88,000` paid to the winner plus `5,000` commission. Keep this example as a canonical test fixture while making the calculation inputs configurable.

### 6.3 Auction validation rules

- Only eligible members who have not already won can bid.
- Members in arrears cannot bid, according to the source concept.
- Bids cannot exceed the configured cap.
- A tie is resolved by the configured rule, such as earliest valid bid or a random draw.
- Auction close is automatic at the deadline.
- If there are no bids, use the configured fallback, such as a lottery or a default mode.
- Sealed bids remain hidden until close; open-ascending bidding is an optional live-demo mode.

### 6.4 Required tests

- Winning discount is within the configured cap.
- Commission and dividend pool reconcile to the winning discount.
- Per-member dividend totals reconcile to the pool, including any configured rounding rule.
- The same input always produces the same settlement output.
- A winner cannot win a second time.
- An ineligible or late member cannot place a valid bid.
- A no-bid cycle follows the configured fallback.
- Final-cycle behavior awards the pot to the remaining member without a bid.
- Duplicate payment webhooks or repeated actions do not duplicate ledger entries or financial actions.
- Every settlement can be reproduced from its stored inputs and rule version.

**Decision still required:** define currency minor-unit rounding, treatment of unequal contributions, partial payments, failed/late payments at auction close, refunds, and any exception to “installment = chit value / members.” Do not hide these cases inside UI logic.

## 7. AI agents and risk signals

AI agents should use structured tools and verified product data, not invent amounts or rules.

### Collections agent

- Drafts friendly reminders and proposes a staged escalation.
- Proposes a catch-up invoice when permitted.
- Alerts the organizer when normal steps fail.
- Never marks a person paid without confirmed payment evidence.
- Does not send or create a new financial request without the required approval.

### Bid advisor

- Explains what a proposed discount would mean for the winner's payout.
- Compares it with the circle's historical winning bids where enough data exists.
- Clearly labels any range as historical context, not a guarantee of a winning bid.
- Does not place bids for members.

### Ledger explainer

- Answers from stored ledger events and the deterministic settlement result.
- Shows the formula and exact amounts used.
- Says when the record does not contain enough evidence to answer.

### Organizer copilot

- Summarizes late contributions, open approvals, upcoming cycle events, pending security checks, disputes, and exposure.
- Links each alert to the record that supports it.

### Risk scoring for the prototype

The source recommends a transparent, rule-based score because real default data is not available. Inputs may include payment timing, current obligations, whether the member has already received the pot, bidding behavior, completed cycles, and guarantor/collateral coverage. Outputs may include an organizer alert, a suggested security level, and context for bid advice.

**Safety rule:** risk scores must be presented with reason codes and must not be described as objective creditworthiness. They should prompt human review, not automatically deny membership, set punitive terms, or trigger a payout. Do not train or claim a validated predictive model from synthetic data alone.

## 8. PayPal integration plan

The source maps product needs to these PayPal capabilities. Each path must be tested in the sandbox before it is described as working.

| Product need | Potential PayPal path from the source | Build note |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring contributions | Subscriptions and billing plans | Validate creation, cancellation, retries, and payment-status updates. |
| Late contribution / catch-up | Invoices, including partial payment and QR-code support | Tie each invoice to a member, cycle, and obligation. |
| Recording installments | Orders and payments | Reconcile confirmed transactions to ledger events. |
| Statements and transaction history | Reporting and transaction history | Store relevant references; do not rely only on a dashboard screenshot. |
| Payment disagreement | Disputes | Keep ChitCircle's internal dispute log separate from PayPal dispute state. |
| Refund | Refund operations | Record the reason and link the refund to the original transaction. |
| Cross-border circles | Multi-currency account capabilities | Treat multi-currency behavior as unverified until tested. |
| Paying the winning member | Not confirmed in the source | Validate payout support early. If unavailable, use the source's disclosed demo fallback and do not present it as a real payout. |

Technical requirements:
- Build token refresh into the integration layer because sandbox access tokens expire.
- Use idempotency keys or equivalent duplicate protection for payment and settlement actions.
- Verify webhook authenticity and handle retries/out-of-order events.
- Keep payment state separate from the app's financial ledger state, then reconcile them.
- Test success, pending, failure, cancellation, refund, duplicate callback, and dispute cases with Postman or an equivalent test collection.
- Keep credentials server-side; never expose PayPal secrets to the browser.

## 9. Proposed system design

This is a product-level design recommendation based on the components in the concept document. It is not a record of an already built system.

### Frontend

- Organizer dashboard and approval inbox.
- Circle setup and rules summary.
- Member view.
- Cycle / auction workspace.
- Append-only ledger and dispute review.
- Member-by-cycle timeline.

### Backend services

1. **Identity and permissions:** organizer/member roles and circle-level access checks.
2. **Circle configuration:** members, cycles, payout mode, rules, jurisdiction profile, visibility, and consent settings.
3. **Settlement engine:** pure calculations with versioned inputs and unit tests.
4. **Auction service:** eligibility, bid handling, close, tie-break, and no-bid fallback.
5. **Ledger service:** append-only events, linked correction/review records, event provenance.
6. **Contribution service:** PayPal integration, payment matching, invoice/subscription management, and reconciliation.
7. **Security workflow:** guarantor/collateral status and payout gates.
8. **Risk service:** explainable rule-based signals for the demo.
9. **Agent layer:** collections, bid advice, explanations, and organizer summary using scoped tool calls.
10. **Scheduler / workflow:** cycle opening, auction deadlines, reminders, and settlement jobs.
11. **Simulator:** deterministic synthetic members and a fast-forward mode for the demo.

### Suggested technology direction in the source

- Render for backend hosting, database, and scheduled workflows.
- Bryntum for the member-by-cycle timeline.
- Postman for PayPal and settlement API testing.
- Zapier MCP as a possible later connection for reminders and approvals.
- APIMatic as an optional aid for PayPal API context during development.
- AG Grid / AG Studio or Elastic only if they solve a clear need; avoid overlapping dashboard tools.

**Build-depth recommendation:** prioritize Render, the timeline, and API tests. Add extra sponsor tools only after the core settlement and collection path works.

### Core records to model

At minimum, represent:

- User
- Circle
- Circle membership and role
- Rule set / jurisdiction profile
- Cycle
- Contribution obligation
- Payment / PayPal transaction reference
- Invoice or subscription reference
- Bid
- Auction result
- Settlement result and rule version
- Dividend allocation
- Payout / release state
- Guarantor or collateral check
- Ledger event
- Reminder / agent proposal / approval
- Dispute and resolution
- Risk assessment with reason codes
- Member consent / rule acceptance

Store money as integer minor units with a currency code; never use floating-point numbers for financial calculations. Store timestamps with a clear timezone policy. Keep an immutable record of the settlement inputs and versioned rules used for each cycle.

## 10. Scope for the hackathon prototype

### Must have — build these first

- Settlement engine with automated tests.
- Auction service and bid validation.
- Append-only ledger.
- Contribution collection flow through a tested PayPal subscription or invoice path.
- Collections workflow for late contributions.
- Guarantor/collateral workflow before a payout is marked released.
- Organizer dashboard and member view sufficient to show the core flow.
- Simulator or seed data to demonstrate a complete circle without waiting months.

### Should have — add after the must-haves work

- Bid advisor.
- Ledger explainer.
- Rule-based risk score with reasons.
- Agreed-discount early payout.
- Tiered visibility.
- Organizer copilot summary.

### Stretch — do not let these delay the core path

- Live open-ascending auction mode.
- Advance with interest.
- Multiple jurisdiction profiles beyond the primary demo profile.
- Broad Zapier reach.
- Optional AG Studio dashboard.
- Fully validated cross-border currency and payout paths.

### Not in scope for the hackathon prototype

- Claiming that ChitCircle is a licensed chit operator or lender.
- Holding member funds itself.
- Unsupervised AI movement of money.
- A trained default prediction model claimed to work in real life.
- A claim that ChitCircle builds formal credit history; the source explicitly identifies this as a gap relative to some existing services.
- A broad marketplace for finding or joining unknown circles.

## 11. Demo narrative

The demo should prove that ChitCircle is more than a spreadsheet. Use the concept document's sequence:

1. Create a circle, invite members, and show the selected rules / jurisdiction profile.
2. Fast-forward the simulator through multiple cycles while the timeline fills in.
3. Run an auction and show the winning bid, commission, dividend pool, and exact settlement calculation.
4. Make one member miss a payment; show a polite reminder, proposed catch-up invoice, organizer alert, and one-tap approval.
5. Run an agreed-discount early payout and show the effective-rate explanation.
6. Show the winner's guarantor/security check blocking release until verified.
7. Close with the product outcome: replace many repetitive manual tasks with a small number of clear approvals.

Treat “about 40 manual tasks a month to 3 approvals” as a pitch hypothesis from the source, not as a measured result, unless the team measures it in a real workflow test.

## 12. Product success measures

The source gives the intended outcome but does not define measured product KPIs. The following are recommended validation measures:

- **Organizer workload:** time spent per cycle and number of manual follow-ups.
- **Collection performance:** share of contributions paid on time; time from due date to resolution.
- **Settlement correctness:** all supported calculation tests pass; no unexplained difference between input rules and output amounts.
- **Transparency:** members can explain their own installment and payout from the app without organizer help.
- **Dispute handling:** number of ledger disputes, resolution time, and repeat issues.
- **Payment reliability:** successful reconciliation rate and duplicate-action prevention.
- **Approval quality:** proposed actions accepted, rejected, or edited by organizers; false or unsupported recommendations.
- **Safety:** zero money-moving actions executed without required approval; zero privacy leaks between member accounts.

Do not treat synthetic simulator results as evidence of real-world default reduction or financial inclusion impact.

## 13. Security, privacy, and compliance boundaries

- Use a jurisdiction profile to control permitted payout modes, discount caps, commissions, security requirements, and other group-level rules.
- Have a qualified reviewer confirm current rules before presenting legal limits as facts. The source itself says some India-specific limits and amendments must be verified and state laws also apply.
- Interest-bearing advances require separate review because they may be treated as lending.
- Keep personal risk details private to the member and authorized organizer roles.
- Explain why a risk or security flag was raised.
- Do not publicly shame late payers; show payment status with configurable visibility.
- Require explicit consent to circle rules and any member vote that the group configuration requires.
- Secure secrets, verify payment events, log privileged actions, and apply role-based access checks on the server.
- Keep the product wording accurate: reduce operational burden and improve visibility; do not imply removal of legal or financial responsibility.

## 14. Open decisions that should be closed early

The concept document leaves these items unresolved or annotated for discussion:

1. **Payout feasibility:** Which PayPal sandbox path can actually send the winning pot to a member? Test this first.
2. **Commission semantics:** Confirm whether commission is a percentage of chit value or discount, then encode the rule per jurisdiction. The worked example uses 5,000 commission on a 100,000 chit value and 12,000 discount.
3. **Agreed discount:** Is the allowed discount fixed by the circle, negotiated for each person, or bounded by a group-approved range?
4. **Security policy:** Is guarantor/collateral required for every winner or only when the configured risk / jurisdiction rule demands it?
5. **Advance with interest:** If ever included, choose the interest calculation method and require legal review. Keep this out of the first demo unless necessary.
6. **Visibility defaults:** Which statuses are shared with the group, and can a group change these defaults without revealing private risk information?
7. **Payment edge cases:** Define partial payments, failed payments, late payments near auction close, refunds, and duplicate or out-of-order payment events.
8. **Consent and disputes:** Define who can vote, what approval threshold is required, and how a disputed ledger entry affects the cycle.
9. **Primary demo community:** Choose one community and rule profile for the main story; use a second diaspora example only if it helps prove configurability.
10. **Name and positioning:** ChitCircle is a working name; do a brand and product-name check before public launch.

## 15. Build sequence

### Day 1–2: prove the risky integrations

- Test PayPal orders, invoices, subscriptions, token refresh, and payout feasibility.
- Test the member timeline with synthetic data.
- Set up a deployable backend skeleton.
- Choose the single primary demo payout mode and rule profile.

### Week 1: make the money math correct

- Implement settlement as a pure function.
- Add canonical example tests and edge-case tests.
- Build the ledger schema and event-writing service.
- Implement auction validation and close behavior.
- Implement the narrowest usable PayPal collection path.

### Week 2: build the organizer workflow

- Implement dashboard, circle setup, member view, and timeline.
- Add contribution matching and the collections workflow.
- Add the guarantor/security state machine.
- Build the simulator and seeded demo data.

### Week 3: improve trust and demo clarity

- Add the explainer and bid advisor if core flows are stable.
- Add rule-based risk reasons and tiered visibility.
- Add external reminder tooling only if the internal workflow already works.
- Run API checks, review privacy/access rules, capture the demo, and prepare the submission.
- Keep the final two days for stabilization, demo, write-up, and submission rather than new features.

## 16. Definition of done for the demo

The prototype is demo-ready when:

- A circle can be configured and members can be represented in the system.
- A cycle can collect or simulate confirmed contributions and update the ledger.
- A valid auction can close and produce a reproducible settlement.
- The canonical worked example reconciles exactly.
- A late contribution triggers a proposed reminder and an approved next step.
- Security verification prevents premature payout release.
- A member can see their own transaction history and receive a ledger-backed explanation.
- A dispute can be recorded without rewriting the original ledger event.
- PayPal capabilities are described exactly as tested, with sandbox limitations clearly stated.
- The full story can be shown with simulated time rather than waiting for real monthly cycles.

## 17. Final product direction

The strongest version of ChitCircle is not “AI for chit funds.” It is **a rule-driven savings-circle system that gives organizers back their time and gives members evidence they can check**. Lead with one complete, correct journey: collect contributions, handle one late payment, run a fair auction, show exact settlement math, verify security, and explain the result from the ledger. Add more payout modes and more agent features only after that journey is trustworthy.
